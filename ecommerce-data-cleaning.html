Raw Sales Data

Data Overview

The original dataset contains approximately 50,000 transaction records, including transaction, date, product, store, customer, quantity, discount and return information.

The raw data was reviewed before performing validation and cleaning checks.

Data Quality Report

The dataset was reviewed for duplicate records, missing values, invalid references and inconsistent data.

General Data Checks

  • Transaction ID: No duplicate transaction IDs were identified.
  • Date: No missing date values were identified.
  • Quantity: Values were within the observed range of 1–4.
  • Returned: The returned field was reviewed as part of the data-quality assessment.

Problems Identified

The following issues were identified and documented during the data-quality checks.

1. Missing Customer IDs

Finding: 1,844 sales records had missing customer IDs.

Action: Records were retained and flagged for further investigation rather than deleted.

2. Missing Discount Values

Finding: 2,583 discount values were blank. The observed discount range was 0 to 0.3, and 29,907 records had a zero discount.

Action: Blank discount values were retained and flagged instead of being replaced with an assumed value.

3. Unmatched Product ID

Finding: Product IDs were compared with the product master table using XLOOKUP. P99999 was identified as an unmatched product ID.

Action: The record was retained and flagged for investigation rather than deleted.

4. Unmatched Store ID

Finding: Store IDs were compared with the store master table. S999 was identified as an unmatched store ID.

Action: The record was retained and flagged for further investigation.

Data Quality Summary

The review identified four areas requiring attention: missing customer IDs, missing discount values, an unmatched product ID and an unmatched store ID.

Flagging these records preserves the original data while making quality issues visible for further investigation and validation.

03 · Key Business Insights

Highlights derived from the dashboard analysis.

1. Profit Margins

Products with 50%+ profit margins generate most of the revenue and sales, making them key drivers of profitability.

2. Loss-Making Products

Loss-making products drive significant sales volume but generate less revenue, indicating a need to review pricing and discount strategies.

3. Low-Margin Products

Products with 0–50% profit margins contribute little to revenue and sales, suggesting a need to review their pricing and performance.

4. Full-Price Sales

Most revenue and orders come from full-price sales, showing strong customer demand without relying on discounts.

5. Discount Impact

As discounts increase, revenue and orders decline, suggesting that deeper discounts may reduce revenue without significantly boosting sales.

6. Seasonal Stability

All major product categories generate consistent revenue across seasons, indicating stable year-round demand.

7. Seasonal Product Performance

Accessories sell best in Winter, Shoes in Spring and Summer, and Tops in Fall, helping optimize seasonal inventory planning.

8. Overall Business Strategy

The business relies on full-price, high-margin sales, while deeper discounts reduce revenue and orders, suggesting a need to minimize unnecessary promotions.
